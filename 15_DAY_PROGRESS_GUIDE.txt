VARUNAPATH AI — 15-DAY PROGRESS PRESENTATION GUIDE
Team: Novara

Use this sequence only for activities your team can demonstrate or document.

Days 1–2: Problem understanding
- Studied overseas bulk-cargo procurement and vessel-chartering workflow.
- Identified cost, delay, congestion, inventory and vessel-capacity problems.

Days 3–4: Existing-system research
- Reviewed freight benchmarks, AIS vessel intelligence and voyage-management platforms.
- Identified the gap: procurement, forecasting and port/vessel optimization are disconnected.

Days 5–6: User requirements
- Defined users: procurement, chartering, inventory and logistics teams.
- Finalized inputs, decisions, constraints and expected outputs.

Days 7–8: Architecture and algorithm design
- Designed Data → Forecast → Optimize → Explain → Monitor workflow.
- Defined demand, capacity, deadline, port-compatibility and risk constraints.

Days 9–10: Dataset preparation
- Prepared simulated demand, supplier, vessel and East Coast port datasets.
- Added cargo price, capacity, transit, waiting time and risk fields.

Day 11: AI forecasting
- Implemented a baseline Linear Regression demand forecast.
- Added a three-month interactive forecast chart.

Day 12: Optimization engine
- Built constraint-based supplier–vessel–port plan generation.
- Added total landed-cost, utilization, waiting and risk calculations.

Day 13: Dashboard integration
- Added command-center metrics, recommendation summary and plan comparison.

Day 14: Disruption simulation
- Added fuel-price, demand-shock and unavailable-port scenarios.
- Implemented automatic re-optimization and alternative recommendations.

Day 15: Testing and documentation
- Checked feasible/infeasible deadlines and multiple disruption scenarios.
- Prepared the SIH presentation, demo flow and technical explanation.

30-SECOND PROGRESS STATEMENT
“During the last 15 days, we moved from domain research and requirement analysis
to a functional proof of concept. We prepared simulated maritime datasets,
implemented a baseline cargo-demand forecast, built a constraint-based optimizer,
and integrated the results into an interactive dashboard. The prototype can
compare supplier–vessel–port combinations and automatically re-optimize the plan
when demand, fuel price or port availability changes.”
